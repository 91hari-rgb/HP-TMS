Who does what, from order to cash and from carrier invoice to payment

Three swimlane diagrams cover the end-to-end process described in chapter 6 of the functional specification. Each lane is a role; each box shows the step, and the orange line shows the file status and track & trace codes it produces.

A. Order to dispatch

An order arrives, is priced and checked, optionally consolidated with similar orders, then planned onto a trip and dispatched.

Instant quote rates freight, service-level and fuel surcharges, border fees and tolls, applies the tax code for the lane and works out the promised delivery from the destination country's calendar.
Margin guardrail holds any order below 12% margin (hold reason H02) until Finance approves it.
Consolidation groups confirmed orders with the same zones, equipment and day into a master order; each child keeps its own charges and invoice.

B. Execution to cash

The driver runs the trip in the app, the customer follows it live, the dispatcher debriefs it, and Finance invoices and collects.

Driver app works offline; each tap logs a T&T code with device time and GPS, synced when the signal returns.
Return orders are created automatically for partial or refused deliveries, on the reversed lane, and go back to planning.
After invoicing Finance handles credit notes and disputes; overdue invoices raise alerts and feed receivables aging.

C. Carrier invoice to payment

Subcontracted trips are accrued at dispatch, the carrier's invoice is matched line by line, and approved invoices are paid through the ERP.

Matching uses our order number when the carrier quotes it; otherwise lane, date, weight and amount, auto-matched at 80% confidence or higher.
Tolerance is ±2% or AED 50, whichever is larger. Over-tolerance lines can be accepted, or disputed and paid at the accrual.
HP TMS does not pay: it exports payment batches, and the ERP's remittance marks invoices paid.
